    How GLP-1 Microdosing Works in Holladay

    Microdosing is a dosing strategy, not a separate drug. The medicine is the same semaglutide or tirzepatide, taken below the label's starting dose or stepped up more gradually, acting on the same receptors. Some people notice quieter food noise. Low doses usually require compounded medicine, which lacks FDA approval.

    Who GLP-1 Microdosing Is For

    Three groups ask about it most. The typical candidates had trouble with nausea on standard doses, prefer a gentler start or are trying to hold onto weight they already lost. It is not a shortcut. Claims that microdosing treats inflammation or aging are not backed by strong evidence.

    Low-Dose Schedule in Holladay

    There is no single standard schedule. Providers often start below the usual first dose, such as a fraction of the 0.25 mg semaglutide or 2.5 mg tirzepatide starting dose, and increase in smaller steps than the label calls for. Some split the weekly amount. Any change should go through your provider first.

    What to Expect Week by Week

    Early weeks vary a lot between people. At low doses, one person may feel full sooner by week two while another feels no difference until the dose is nudged up. Side effects still happen. Expect modest, gradual change.

    Side Effects in Holladay

    Lower doses reduce side effects but do not erase them. Nausea, constipation, diarrhea, reflux and reduced appetite are still the most common, especially in the days after any dose increase, even a small one. The boxed warning remains. Pancreatitis, gallbladder problems and the thyroid C-cell tumor warning from rodent studies apply at any dose.

    Eligibility and Screening

    Screening is the same as for full doses. The review covers BMI, weight history, diagnoses, medications and earlier GLP-1 use, with questions about the dose you reached and what made you stop. MEN 2 is a hard stop. Past pancreatitis or an eating disorder needs a longer conversation.

    Microdosing Compared With Standard GLP-1 Treatment in Holladay

    Standard dosing has the trial data. The STEP and SURMOUNT trials tested full doses and reported average losses near 15 to 20 percent, and nothing comparable exists for low-dose plans. Effects are likely more modest. Maintenance is a common use case.
